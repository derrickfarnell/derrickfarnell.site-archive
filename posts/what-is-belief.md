We all know what it means to believe something. Indeed, belief is one of the most basic concepts of human psychology. Yet it can be surprisingly difficult to define, like time. As the philosopher Augustine wrote of time around AD 400: 'What then is time? If no one asks me, I know; if I wish to explain it to someone who asks, I do not know'.

Any definition of belief must recognise that belief is a psychological state involving a claim – _what_ is believed. For example, we may believe that _humans are apes_, or that _there's no milk in the fridge_.

The term _claim_ can be either a noun, as in _a claim_, or a verb, as in _to claim_. Whereas to claim something is to assert it, whether in speech or writing, a claim needn't have been asserted. For example, if we invent a claim and present it to others _as_ invented, we haven't asserted it. And a claim can be believed without anyone asserting it at the time, not even the believer, as when we look inside our fridge and form the belief _There's no milk in the fridge_.

The term _belief_ also needs clarifying, as it can mean either the psychological state or the believed claim – that is, believing something or something believed. Consider two people looking in the same fridge at the same time and both forming the belief _There's no milk in the fridge_. In the first sense, their beliefs are different things: separate psychological states in different heads. But in the second sense, their beliefs are the same thing: the same claim.

Some claims are mundane, such as _There's no milk in the fridge_, others significant, such as _Humans are apes_. So _mundane belief_ can mean either believing a mundane claim or a mundane claim that's believed, and likewise for _significant belief_. However, _belief_ by itself often means a significant belief, whether moral, political, religious, historical, philosophical or scientific. For example, defending our beliefs usually means either defending particular significant claims or defending our right to believe and act on them. This series, though, concerns believing any claim, significant or mundane.

Belief isn't the only psychological state involving a claim: we can also hope, fear, doubt or imagine that there's no milk in the fridge. So what distinguishes belief? One answer can seem as irrefutable as it is simple: belief is considering a claim to be true. But this definition is fatally flawed – as I'll explain in the next article.
